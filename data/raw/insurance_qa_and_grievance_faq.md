## SECTION 11: HEALTH INSURANCE CONSUMER CLAIMS FAQS

### Clause 11.1: Cashless Claim Authorization Process
Under cashless hospitalization, the network hospital sends a pre-authorization request to the TPA or insurer. The insurer must issue an initial approval or query within 1 hour of receiving complete medical details.

### Clause 11.2: Treatment in Non-Network Hospital
If an insured is admitted to a non-network hospital, the expenses must first be paid out-of-pocket by the policyholder and subsequently claimed through reimbursement within 30 days of discharge.

### Clause 11.3: Grace Period Continuity Benefits
If premium is paid within the 30-day grace period, all accrued benefits including waiting period credits for pre-existing diseases and cumulative bonuses are fully preserved without any penalty.

### Clause 11.4: Pre-Existing Disease Disclosure Norms
Failure to disclose pre-existing diseases at the time of policy proposal permits the insurer to reject claims arising within the first 60 months of policy inception on grounds of non-disclosure.

### Clause 11.5: Free-Look Cancellation Refund Deductions
When a policyholder cancels a policy during the 15-day free-look period, the insurer refunds the premium after deducting proportionate risk premium, medical examination costs, and stamp duty.

### Clause 11.6: Deductible vs Co-Payment Distinction
A deductible is a fixed rupee amount (e.g., ₹10,000) borne first by the insured per policy year. A co-payment is a fixed percentage (e.g., 10%) shared on every admissible claim amount.

### Clause 11.7: Day Care Procedures Hospitalization Criteria
Day care procedures do not require 24 hours of hospital confinement if advanced medical technology enables discharge within 24 hours in a registered daycare surgical facility.

### Clause 11.8: AYUSH Hospitalization Recognition
AYUSH treatments are admissible only if taken in a recognized government AYUSH hospital or an accredited teaching hospital under the Clinical Establishments Act.

### Clause 11.9: Pre-Hospitalization Diagnostic Tests Coverage
Diagnostic tests (such as MRI, CT scans, blood work) conducted within 60 days before hospital admission are reimbursable if directly related to the inpatient hospitalization condition.

### Clause 11.10: Post-Hospitalization Follow-up Pharmacy Bills
Pharmacy bills and follow-up consultation fees incurred within 180 days post-discharge are payable provided prescriptions from the treating medical practitioner are attached.

## SECTION 12: MOTOR INSURANCE CONSUMER CLAIMS FAQS

### Clause 12.1: Compulsory Deductible Engine Thresholds
Under Indian Motor Tariff regulations, private cars with engine capacity up to 1,500 cc carry a compulsory deductible of ₹1,000; cars exceeding 1,500 cc carry a compulsory deductible of ₹2,000.

### Clause 12.2: Mandatory FIR for Vehicle Theft
In the event of motor vehicle theft, immediate registration of a First Information Report (FIR) with the police is compulsory. An insurance claim cannot be settled without an untraced report.

### Clause 12.3: Zero Depreciation Add-on Validity
A Zero Depreciation (bumper-to-bumper) add-on waives depreciation deductions on plastic, rubber, metal, and glass parts, but is generally available only for vehicles up to 5 years of age.

### Clause 12.4: Third Party Property Damage Statutory Limit
The statutory liability for third-party property damage under the Motor Vehicles Act is capped at ₹7,50,000 per accident. Unlimited liability applies only to third-party death or bodily injury.

### Clause 12.5: Transfer of No Claim Bonus to Replacement Car
When a car owner sells their vehicle, the earned No Claim Bonus (NCB) can be retained via an NCB Reserving Letter and transferred to a new car purchased within 3 years.

### Clause 12.6: Water Ingression and Hydrostatic Lock Damage
If a vehicle engine is stalled in floodwaters, attempting to crank or restart the engine causes hydrostatic lock, which is classified as consequential damage and excluded under base cover.

### Clause 12.7: Constructive Total Loss 75% IDV Rule
When the estimated repair and retrieval cost exceeds 75% of the vehicle's Insured Declared Value (IDV), the claim is settled on a Constructive Total Loss basis, paying out the full IDV.

### Clause 12.8: Compulsory Personal Accident Owner-Driver Exemption
An owner-driver is legally exempt from purchasing the mandatory ₹15 Lakhs CPA cover if they already hold an independent 24-hour personal accident policy with at least ₹15 Lakhs sum insured.

### Clause 12.9: Driving License Disqualification Impact
Operating an insured vehicle with an expired or invalid driving license, or under statutory intoxication (>30 mg alcohol/100 ml blood), voids all Own Damage and Third Party insurance cover.

### Clause 12.10: Surveyor Deputation for Losses Exceeding ₹50,000
Motor vehicle claims estimated above ₹50,000 must be physically surveyed and assessed by an independent IRDAI-licensed insurance surveyor before repairs begin.

## SECTION 13: GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL AND OMBUDSMAN FAQS

### Clause 13.1: Grievance Officer Escalation Timeline
An insurer's Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO) must resolve written policyholder complaints within 14 calendar days of receipt.

### Clause 13.2: Approaching the Insurance Ombudsman
A complainant can approach the Insurance Ombudsman only after the insurer has rejected the complaint, provided an unsatisfactory response, or failed to reply within 30 days.

### Clause 13.3: Ombudsman Monetary Limit ₹30 Lakhs
The Insurance Ombudsman has legal jurisdiction to adjudicate insurance disputes where the total claim amount plus compensation sought does not exceed ₹30,00,000.

### Clause 13.4: One-Year Limitation for Ombudsman Complaints
A complaint to the Insurance Ombudsman must be lodged within 1 year from the date on which the insurer issued its written rejection or final decision.

### Clause 13.5: Penal Interest on Delayed Claims
If an insurer delays claim disbursement beyond 30 days of receiving the final required document, the insurer must pay penal interest at 2% above the prevailing RBI Bank Rate.

### Clause 13.6: Binding Nature of Ombudsman Decision
The Ombudsman's award is legally binding on the insurance company within 30 days of acceptance by the policyholder, but does not prevent the policyholder from moving Consumer Courts if dissatisfied.
