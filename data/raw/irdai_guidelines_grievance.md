# IRDAI POLICYHOLDER PROTECTION AND GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL GUIDELINES

**Regulatory Authority:** Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)  
**Regulation Title:** IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests and Allied Operations) Regulations, 2024  
**Document Reference:** IRDAI/REG/PPHI/2024/03  
**Applicability:** All General, Life, and Health Insurance Companies operating in India  

---

## SECTION 1: POINT OF SALE AND POLICY ISSUANCE STANDARDS

### Clause 1.1: Free-Look Cancellation Period
A policyholder of a personal health or life insurance policy is entitled to a Free-Look Period of 15 days from the date of receipt of the physical policy document (or 30 days in case of policies obtained through distance marketing or electronic modes) to review the terms and conditions. If the insured disagrees with any clause, they may return the policy stating the reasons, upon which the insurer must refund the premium paid after deducting proportionate risk premium for the period on cover, medical examination expenses incurred by the insurer, and stamp duty charges.

### Clause 1.2: Minimum Grace Period Standards
Every individual life and health insurance policy shall provide a Grace Period of:
1. 30 days for policies with annual, half-yearly, or quarterly premium payment modes.
2. 15 days for policies with monthly premium payment frequency.
During the grace period, coverage remains valid, and the insurer cannot deny an admissible claim. If a claim arises during the grace period, the overdue installment premium may be deducted from the claim settlement amount.

### Clause 1.3: Prohibition of Unilateral Mid-Term Alteration
An insurer shall not alter the terms, conditions, premium rates, or exclusions of an insurance policy during the subsistence of the policy term, except where permitted under specific IRDAI guidelines or statutory tax modifications.

### Clause 1.4: Policy Portability Rights
Every health insurance policyholder has the statutory right to port their individual or family floater health policy from one insurer to another at renewal. The porting application must be submitted to the new insurer at least 45 days prior to the policy expiry date. The acquiring insurer must credit accrued continuity benefits including waiting periods for pre-existing diseases and cumulative bonuses up to the sum insured of the previous policy.

## SECTION 2: CLAIMS PROCESSING AND SETTLEMENT TIMELINES

### Clause 2.1: Initial Query on Claim Documentation
An insurer shall raise all queries or requests for supplementary documentation related to a claim in a single communication within 15 days of receiving the initial claim intimation. Piezomeal or staggered document requests are strictly prohibited under IRDAI regulations.

### Clause 2.2: Mandatory Claim Turnaround Time (TAT)
The insurer shall adjudicate, settle, or reject a claim within 30 days from the date of receipt of the final required document or surveyor's report. 

### Clause 2.3: Special Investigation in Suspicious Claims
Where the circumstances of a claim warrant an in-depth investigation for suspected misrepresentation or fraud, the insurer shall initiate the investigation immediately and conclude it within 30 days. The ultimate decision on claim settlement must not exceed 45 days from the date of original receipt of claim documents.

### Clause 2.4: Statutory Interest on Delayed Claim Payment
In the event of a delay in the payment of an admissible claim beyond the 30-day timeline mandated in Clause 2.2, the insurer shall be liable to pay penal interest to the policyholder at a rate 2% above the prevailing Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Bank Rate from the date of receipt of the last document until the actual date of payment.

## SECTION 3: GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL MECHANISM AND OMBUDSMAN JURISDICTION

### Clause 3.1: Insurer Internal Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO)
Every insurance company must designate a Chief Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO) at its head office and branch grievance officers at each operating branch. A policyholder grievance submitted in writing or by email must be acknowledged within 3 business days and resolved with a reasoned written order within 14 calendar days of receipt.

### Clause 3.2: IRDAI Bima Bharosa Portal (Formerly IGMS)
If a policyholder does not receive a response from the insurer's GRO within 14 days, or is dissatisfied with the insurer's resolution, the policyholder may escalate the complaint to the IRDAI through the 'Bima Bharosa' online portal (toll-free: 155255 / 1800 4254 732).

### Clause 3.3: Eligibility and Jurisdiction of Insurance Ombudsman
A policyholder may approach the Insurance Ombudsman having territorial jurisdiction if:
1. The grievance was first rejected by the insurer, or no resolution was provided within 30 days of submission to the GRO.
2. The total claim value, including compensation sought, does not exceed ₹30,00,000 (Rupees Thirty Lakhs).
3. The complaint is filed before the Ombudsman within one year from the date of receipt of the insurer's rejection order.
4. No complaint on the same matter is pending before any court of law, consumer commission, or arbitrator.

### Clause 3.4: Binding Nature of Ombudsman Award
An award passed by the Insurance Ombudsman is binding on the insurance company. The insurer must comply with the award within 30 days of receiving the policyholder's letter of acceptance. However, if the policyholder is dissatisfied with the Ombudsman's award, they retain the full legal right to approach Consumer Courts (DCDRC, SCDRC, NCDRC) or civil courts.
